# One-point standard — how to write so others do it right

## One sheet, one point, one picture

A long work standard will not be read. A one-point lesson has: one thing to do, one point that is easy to get wrong, one photo or sketch of exactly that spot. No procedure, no history, no long explanation.

## Write backwards from the error

The fastest way to write it is to start from the error that just happened: what happened, where the person doing it misunderstood, how to spot it immediately. A standard born from a real error carries more weight than ten standards copied from documents.

## Three lines it must have

- What to do, in what order (three steps at most).
- What right looks like, what wrong looks like.
- What to do if unsure (stop and ask whom).

The third is the most often dropped, and it is also the one that stops an error spreading across batches.

## Verify before issuing

Hand the sheet to someone who has never done the job and ask them to follow it, with no extra explanation. Anywhere that person has to ask is a place to fix in the standard, not their fault. This is the only test for a new standard.

## After issuing

Put it at the workstation, not in a document box. Write the review date on the sheet itself. When the standard and reality differ, the thing to fix is either the standard or the way of working — not both at once, and you must record which one you fixed.
