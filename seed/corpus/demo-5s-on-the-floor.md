# 5S — start where mistakes are easiest, not with a slogan

## Pick one area, not the whole plant

A plant-wide 5S campaign usually ends up as one big clean-up. A more solid way: pick a small area with an obvious problem — a place where tools go missing, or an aisle that keeps getting blocked — and work it until the people there say "it really is easier to work here".

## Five steps, in this exact order

1. Sort: take everything out of the area that is not used for the job running now. Do not throw it away — put it in a holding area with the date and who left it.
2. Set in order: each thing has one place, the place is marked, and the place is chosen by how often it is used (the ones used most, closest to hand).
3. Shine: cleaning is a way to inspect the machine. If wiping it down turns up a fresh oil trace, that is a find, not lost time.
4. Standardise: turn the three steps above into a habit with an owner and a time inside the shift.
5. Sustain: only once you hold it is it worth talking about discipline and scoring.

## How to check without a paperwork trail

Stand at the entrance to the area and ask anyone: what is this for, how often is it used, who is responsible for it. If they can answer, the 5S is alive. If they cannot, the scoreboard can still look good.

## The biggest trap

Using 5S as a periodic campaign: a hard clean for a week, then back to how it was. The sign it holds is not the scoreboard, but an area that keeps itself in order when no one is checking.
