# Lessons and experience from the work

> **What goes here:** whatever the work taught that travels — a judgement call that held up, a mistake worth not repeating, a rule of thumb about people, meetings, numbers or machines. One idea per line, dated, with the situation it came from. **No plant, product, machine or person names**: this is the part of the vault meant to survive a job change ([[index]]).
> **Who writes it:** the nightly filing run when the day's note holds one, and the Saturday review when a question about it has been answered. The lesson writer refuses a line that names anything local ([[index|the jargon list]] plus every person page), and allows one entry a day unless a second is forced.
> **Promotion:** a theme that appears three times earns its own page, and these lines then point at it. When this page passes 150 lines, the year is archived as `lessons-<year>.md` and a new page starts.

## Entries
- **2026-09-26** — At supervisor level you cannot follow the shop floor minute by minute; the detail has to come up to you through a short daily check-in with the shift leads - capture at the point of occurrence stays with them, consolidation stays with you *From:* settling where the day note is written and where the detail comes from.
- **2026-09-26** — A record nobody must attend to changes nothing: reading is unenforceable, attendance is observable - so put any review where a second person notices whether it happened *From:* research on how experienced managers keep open work moving. `src: documents/2026-09-26-open-work-research.md`
- **2026-09-26** — A measure promoted to a target stops being a good measure: if people are judged on a composite number it improves on the report rather than on the floor, so pair every number with a counter-measure and set the target only after a baseline *From:* designing the weekly scorecard from the operating-rhythm research. `src: documents/2026-09-26-operating-rhythm-research.md`
- **2026-09-28** — Scope a support role by area and write down what it does not cover. A helper expected to cover everything ends up overloaded, and the work falls back to the operators the role was created to relieve *From:* the material-handling role turned out to cover only the automated packing area; the manual areas declare for themselves because carrying the full list would overload one person.
- **2026-09-29** — Set next year's cost estimate beside this year's and explain every step change by the project behind it - an unexplained rise reads as drift and gets cut, an explained one reads as a decision *From:* reviewing next year's operating costs and finding the whole increase was one improvement project everyone already knew about.
- **2026-09-30** — A capability proven on a machine is not capacity: it becomes capacity only when the plan schedules it - treat the proof as information and the schedule change as the work *From:* the technical team reported a machine could now run an extra lane and expected the plan to be optimised to use it.
- **2026-10-02** — Repeated attempts are not a plan: work that only ever gets another try never gets a date, so what is missing is a scheduled window with an owner *From:* an asset had been out of production for nearly a year, the maintenance team had tried several times and would try once more, with no window planned and no date set.
- **2026-10-03** — A measure is only as good as where it is recorded: if work is booked at a shared terminal instead of the machine it was actually done on, every split of that number inherits the error - record it at the point of work or it stops being comparable *From:* Scrap was being declared by several people at one machine's computer and booked to that machine rather than the one the work was done on, so the per-machine and per-product split could not be trusted.
- **2026-10-05** — Never accept an improvement on its intention: require the number it promised and then the same number measured again on the real run, plus a check that output quality, counting accuracy and scrap did not move the wrong way *From:* his manager's instruction that every improvement project coming from the technical side be evaluated on those four points before it counts.
- **2026-10-07** — When a declared figure and the physical measurement of the same thing disagree for months, review the measuring and declaring method before believing the work changed - the deviation may sit in the measurement, not in the behaviour *From:* waste declared in the system and waste actually weighed were off by more than a tenth for two consecutive months, and the first move was to compare the weighing method against last month rather than to act on the extra loss.
- **2026-10-08** — A controlled document found in use at an obsolete revision is a withdrawal failure, not a printing one: ask how the superseded copy survived in the area before reprinting it, or the same copy comes back *From:* an area was found running an old revision of a controlled form while a much later revision was current, and the first move was to find out why the superseded copy was still there rather than simply to reprint.
