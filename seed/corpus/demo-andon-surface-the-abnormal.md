# Andon: surface the abnormal before it becomes an incident

## Andon is not the lamp, it is an agreement

Many plants buy the lamps and still cannot get anyone to come. What is needed first is the agreement: when the operator pulls the andon, who comes, within how long, and whether that person has the authority to stop the line. Without those three, the lamp is just decoration.

## Three levels, not one button

- Light: needs support within the shift, line does not stop. For example, a material shortage at the last station.
- Medium: the line keeps running but risks stopping within tens of minutes — someone with expertise needs to come right away.
- Heavy: quality or safety is already affected — stop the line first, talk afterwards.

Set the levels in advance so the operator does not have to judge for themselves whether something is an abnormality or an incident.

## Who must call, and why people do not

People do not pull the andon because they fear being seen as weak, or because last time they called they got shouted at. Two things to do: say out loud that calling at the right moment is encouraged behaviour, and never use andon calls as evidence in someone's individual appraisal. The only way to know whether the agreement is alive is to count the calls and see whether the response met the deadline.

## Record it after every call

One line: the symptom, who came, how long it took, what was done. This is the only data that shows which abnormality recurs. An abnormality that recurs at the same station is a job for planned maintenance, not for the shift crew.
